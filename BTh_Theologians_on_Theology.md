# THEOLOGIANS ON THEOLOGY
## What Great Thinkers Mean by "Theology" — Explained in Simple English

**Course:** Introduction to Theology (BTh)
**Source:** *An Introduction to Theology*, Indian Theological Series — Rev. Fr. Kuncheria Pathil CMI & Rev. Fr. Dominic Veliath SDB

*This document collects the theologians discussed in the book, explains each one's idea of theology in plain language, gives the exact phrases the book uses (in quotation marks), and adds a concrete example for each.*

## PART ONE: THE CLASSIC WESTERN THEOLOGIANS

### 1. ANSELM OF CANTERBURY — "Faith Seeking Understanding"

**What he means by theology:**
Theology begins with belief and then uses reason to understand that belief. You do not first prove God and then believe; you believe first, and belief itself pushes you to understand more deeply.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology is *"faith seeking understanding"* (Latin: *fides quaerens intellectum*).
- *"I believe in order to understand"* (Latin: *credo ut intelligam*).

**Example:**
A believer already accepts that God exists. Anselm then asks: "Can my reason show *why* it makes sense that God exists?" His faith is not replaced by reason — his faith is the starting engine that drives his reasoning.

---

### 2. AUGUSTINE OF HIPPO — "Believe That You May Understand"

**What he means by theology:**
Very close to Anselm. Understanding is the reward of faith, not its precondition. We reflect on God from the standpoint of a person already being saved.

**Exact phrase:**
- *"Believe that you may understand."*

**Example:**
Augustine studies his own life (his conversion, his restless heart) and reads it as a map of the soul returning to God. He does theology by reflecting on lived experience of grace, not by cold logic alone.

---

### 3. THOMAS AQUINAS — Theology as "Sacred Science"

**What he means by theology:**
Theology is a real *science* (an organized body of knowledge), but a special one. Most sciences start from things reason can see on its own (like mathematics). Theology instead starts from truths God has revealed, and then reasons carefully from them.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology is a *"sacred science"* (Latin: *scientia*); *"it borrows its principles from the science of God made known to us by revelation."*
- He replaced *"the way of discovery (via inventionis)"* with *"the way of exposition (via doctrinae)."*

**What that last phrase means simply:**
- *Way of discovery* = personally searching, wrestling, finding truth step by step.
- *Way of exposition* = laying out the truth in a clear, orderly system once you already have it.
Aquinas built the orderly system (the *Summa*) so faith and reason fit together neatly.

**Example:**
To explain the Incarnation, Aquinas does not just quote the Bible. He borrows principles from revelation, then uses Aristotle's logic to arrange conclusions into a structured, teachable system — like a vast, well-organized encyclopedia of faith.

---

### 4. BONAVENTURE — Theology as a Journey of the Soul

**What he means by theology:**
For Bonaventure, theology is more about *love* than intellect. It is the soul's journey toward God. Philosophy is only a helper along the way, not the goal.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology is *"an itinerary of the soul toward God"* (an *itinerary* = a travel-route).
- *"As the children of Israel carried away the treasures of Egypt... so theologians make their own the teachings of philosophy."*

**Example:**
Bonaventure takes useful ideas from philosophy the way the Israelites took Egyptian gold on their way out — using them, but for a higher purpose: to move the heart closer to God.

---

### 5. JOHN DUNS SCOTUS — Everything Begins in Love

**What he means by theology:**
Theology's object is God, and the deepest truth about God is love. So all reality flows out of divine love.

**Exact phrase:**
- Since *"God is love, everything has its origin in love."*

**Example:**
When Scotus asks why the world exists, or why Christ came, his answer is not "because of sin" but "because of love" — love is the first cause behind creation itself.

---

### 6. WILLIAM OF OCKHAM — Theology Is NOT a Science

**What he means by theology:**
Ockham disagrees with Aquinas. A true science must rest on self-evident principles. But theology rests on revealed truths we *accept on faith*, not truths reason can see by itself. So, strictly speaking, theology is not a science.

**Exact phrase:**
- Theology is *"not a science, because its principles are not evident but accepted from revelation."*

**Example:**
"God is three persons" cannot be proven the way "2 + 2 = 4" can. We hold it because God revealed it. For Ockham, that makes theology a matter of faith, not of scientific proof.

## PART TWO: MODERN AND PROTESTANT VOICES

### 7. KARL BARTH — Theology Speaks Only of God

**What he means by theology:**
Theology's one task is to understand and speak about God — but God as God reveals Himself in the Gospel, not God as humans imagine Him. It must depend on God alone, dropping all outside supports.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology's task is *"to understand and speak of 'God'."*
- It must depend on the *"God of the Gospel"* and *"renounce all external guarantees."*

**Example:**
Barth refuses to base faith on human philosophy, science, or culture as a safety net. He insists: start only from what God has spoken in Christ. No human "guarantee" is allowed to prop it up.

---

### 8. KARL RAHNER — Theology as "Theological Anthropology"

**What he means by theology:**
To talk about God, you must talk about the human being, because the two go together. Humans are built with a limitless openness to the Infinite. So studying human nature is a doorway to studying God.

**Exact phrase:**
- *"God and humanity are correlative terms."* (*Correlative* = they belong together; you cannot fully explain one without the other.)

**Example:**
To explain the Incarnation, Rahner starts with human beings. Human nature is an *"openness to God,"* so we are natural *"awaiters"* of God's word. The Incarnation then becomes the ultimate fulfilment of humanity's deep longing for an *"absolute saviour"* — not a random event, but the answer to a built-in human hope.

---

### 9. PAUL TILLICH — Faith as "Ultimate Concern"

**What he means by theology:**
Faith is whatever you take with total seriousness as the ground of your life. Theology reflects on that ultimate concern.

**Exact phrase:**
- Faith is *"ultimate concern."*

**Example:**
Whatever grips a person most deeply — what they would build their whole life around — functions as their "god." Authentic faith is when that ultimate concern is truly God, not money, nation, or self.

---

### 10. DIETRICH BONHOEFFER — A Theology of Secularisation

**What he means by theology:**
Bonhoeffer argues, provocatively, that truly bringing the Gospel into the world means letting the world be fully itself — mature, responsible, "come of age" — rather than clinging to a religious cover over everything.

**Exact phrase:**
- *"To genuinely Christianise the world is to secularise it."*

**Example:**
Instead of keeping believers dependent on religious rules for every decision, Bonhoeffer wants Christians to live responsibly in the ordinary "secular" world — that, he says, is where real Christian discipleship happens.

---

### 11. THEODORE W. JENNINGS JR. — Theology as Reflection on the "Mythos"

**What he means by theology:**
Theology is disciplined reflection on the Christian story — the great narrative ("mythos") of God, creation, Christ, and salvation that the community lives by.

**Exact phrase:**
- Theology reflects on the Christian *"mythos."*

**Example:**
Rather than starting with abstract definitions, this approach starts with the Christian story itself and thinks carefully about what that story means and demands.

## PART THREE: THE FOUR SELECTED MODELS (Chapter IV)

*The book studies four "models" — four different ways of actually doing theology.*

| Model | Theologian | Core idea in one line |
|-------|-----------|-----------------------|
| Syriac-Semitic | St. Ephraem | Theology as poetry and symbol |
| Scholastic | Thomas Aquinas | Theology as ordered sacred science |
| Transcendental | Karl Rahner | Theology through the human person |
| Liberation | Gutierrez / Kappen | Theology as reflection on action for the poor |

---

### 12. ST. EPHRAEM — Theology as Poetry

**What he means by theology:**
God is limitless, so tight definitions actually distort Him. Better to approach God through paradox, symbol, and poetry, which point beyond the words themselves.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology is *"poetry,"* proceeding *"by way of paradox and symbol."*
- Definitions are arrogant attempts to *"tie down and restrict the Limitless One."*
- Theology is *"the reflection on the human experience of God."*

**Example:**
Ephraem treats nature and the Bible as two complementary *"realms of revelation."* Instead of dissecting God with logic, he writes hymns where visible symbols (light, fire, pearl) open a window onto the hidden God.

---

### 13. THOMAS AQUINAS (as Model) — The Scholastic System

**What he means by theology:** (see also entry 3)
Build a clear, harmonious system where revealed faith and human reason support each other.

**Exact phrase:**
- Theology is a *"sacred science."*

**Example:**
Aquinas borrows starting-points from revelation, then uses Aristotelian logic to draw ordered conclusions, producing structured *Summae* to teach the faith objectively — the *"way of exposition."*

---

### 14. KARL RAHNER (as Model) — Theological Anthropology

**What he means by theology:** (see also entry 8)
Study God by studying the human openness to God.

**Exact phrase:**
- *"God and humanity are correlative terms."*

**Example:**
The Incarnation is explained as the fulfilment of the human being's built-in longing for an *"absolute saviour."*

---

### 15. LIBERATION THEOLOGY — Theology as Reflection on Action

**What they mean by theology:**
Theology is not first about ideas; it is about action for justice, then reflection on that action in the light of God's Word. Start with the struggle against poverty and oppression.

**Exact phrases (Gustavo Gutierrez):**
- Theology is *"a critical reflection on Christian praxis in the light of the Word."* (*Praxis* = committed action / practice.)
- The Bible is read *"from the standpoint of the poor and oppressed."*

**Example:**
Liberation theologians use social and economic analysis to find the root causes of oppression, then read the Exodus story as a real paradigm: just as God freed the slaves from Egypt, God wills to free the oppressed from unjust structures today.

## PART FOUR: INDIAN THEOLOGIANS

*These thinkers "translate" Christian faith into Indian philosophical and spiritual language.*

### 16. BRAHMABANDHAB UPADHYAYA — Christian Faith Through Vedanta

**What he means by theology:**
Just as the early Church Fathers used Greek philosophy to explain the Gospel to the Greek world, Indian theologians should use Vedanta philosophy to explain the Gospel to the Indian mind.

**Exact idea (from the book):**
- We in India must *"introduce the Christian faith in terms of the Vedanta philosophy"* to make it understandable and acceptable to Indian people.

**Example:**
He put on the saffron cloth, lived as a *sannyasi* (renouncer), mastered Vedanta, and worked for an Indian Church with indigenous liturgy and theology.

---

### 17. A. J. APPASAMY — Theology Through Bhakti and Saccidananda

**What he means by theology:**
Do theology through the warm Hindu *Bhakti* tradition of personal devotion to a personal God, rather than through cold, impersonal abstraction.

**Exact idea:**
- He used *Saccidananda* — *Sat* (Being), *Chit* (Consciousness), *Ananda* (Bliss) — to explain the three persons of the Trinity, while keeping *"our separate personalities in our experience of union with God."*

**Example:**
Father = *Sat* (Source of Being); Son = *Chit* (Divine Wisdom); Holy Spirit = *Ananda* (Divine Bliss/Love). Unlike strict non-dualism, Appasamy keeps the believer's personhood intact — God is loved, not simply dissolved into.

---

### 18. PANDIPEDDI CHENCHAIAH — Christ as "New Creation"

**What he means by theology:**
Direct experience of Christ matters more than institutional doctrines. Christ is a new reality breaking into the world.

**Exact phrase:**
- Christ is the *"new creation."*

**Example:**
Rather than defending church structures, Chenchaiah points to the living experience of Christ as a fresh, transforming power in a person's life.

---

### 19. VENGAL CHAKKARAI — Starting from Christ the Avatara

**What he means by theology:**
Do not begin theology with abstract talk about "God." Begin with the concrete experience of Christ, understood through the Indian idea of *avatara* (God taking visible form).

**Exact idea:**
- He *"started his theological reflection not with God, but with the experience of Christ as an avatara."*

**Example:**
Chakkarai reflects on Jesus as God-made-visible in a way Indian hearts already understand through the avatara tradition, then works outward from that living experience.

---

### 20. SWAMI ABHISHIKTANANDA (Henri Le Saux) — The "Cave of the Heart"

**What he means by theology:**
Theology is fundamentally a *word (Logos) about God*, but it must be *experienced within*, not just studied outside. Christians should not seek God only as an external object; they must journey inward.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology is *"fundamentally a word (Logos) concerning God."*
- The inward goal is *"the cave of the heart' — the locus of the Indian experience of the Mystery."*

**Example:**
Through deep contemplation, a believer travels to the deepest, quietest centre of the soul — the "cave of the heart" — and there directly experiences *"divine sonship in the unity of the Spirit."*

---

### 21. SEBASTIAN KAPPEN — Indian Liberation Theology and "Jesuology"

**What he means by theology:**
Build a liberation theology specifically for India, starting not from Western dogmas but from a fresh portrait of Jesus — a *"Jesuology"* — that connects Jesus to India's own history of protest against injustice.

**Exact phrases:**
- He began with a *"new Indian Christology or Jesuology."*
- He placed Jesus in the *"history of socio-religious movements of dissent, contestation and revolt in India."*

**Example:**
Kappen presents Jesus as a social rebel who challenged oppressive religious leaders, comparing this to Indian reform movements (like the Buddha's protest) against rigid, caste-based ritualism.

---

## PART FIVE: EASTERN ORTHODOX VOICE

### 22. ALEXANDER SCHMEMANN — Theology from the Liturgy

**What he means by theology:**
Theology grows out of worship. It puts into words the Church's living experience of God in the liturgy.

**Exact phrases:**
- Theology is *"an explanation, the search for words appropriate to 'Who God is'."*
- It is a *"system of symbols reflecting the Church's experience of God."*

**Example:**
For Schmemann, you learn who God is first by *worshipping* — and theology is the careful attempt to express in words what the community has already tasted in the liturgy.

## STUDY REFLECTION

1. Notice the two great families of definition:
   - **Faith-first** (Anselm, Augustine, Barth): start from what God reveals.
   - **Human-experience-first** (Rahner, Tillich, Abhishiktananda): start from the human person's openness and experience.

2. Notice the Indian contribution: theology is not only *thought* but *experienced* — in the *"cave of the heart,"* in *Bhakti* devotion, in the struggle for justice.

3. Ask yourself: **Which definition of theology feels closest to your own faith experience — and why?**
