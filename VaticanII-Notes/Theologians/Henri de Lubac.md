# Henri de Lubac, SJ

> [!summary] One-line
> Jesuit patristic scholar, founder of **nouvelle théologie**; silenced 10 years then rehabilitated by John XXIII; shaped **People of God** in [[Lumen Gentium]], wary of [[Gaudium et Spes]]' optimism; co-founded **Communio**.

**Order:** Jesuit · **Tendency:** neo-Augustinian / ressourcement · **Journal:** [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics|Communio]]

## Background & method
Founder of **la nouvelle théologie** and the **ressourcement** movement — reaction against "hyper-rationalistic, deductive, a-historical" neo-Scholasticism (manualism). Return *ad fontes* (Scripture + Fathers) with a historical, culturally generous reading of Aquinas. On nature/grace (*le surnaturel*) he was **silenced by the Holy Office for ten years** in the 1950s, then rehabilitated by John XXIII for the Council.

## Key concepts
- **People of God:** shifted ecclesiology from hierarchical/clerical institution to the **"Community of the Whole People of God."**
- **Nature & grace:** deep connection of natural and supernatural, held in a **neo-Augustinian** frame.

## Documents shaped
- **[[Lumen Gentium]]** — People of God ecclesiology (arguably the most important peritus).
- **[[Gaudium et Spes]]** — helped draft it, but grew **skeptical** of its optimistic anthropology; feared a "tide of immanentism" dissolving the Church into the world.

## Post-conciliar trajectory
Broke with the liberal **Concilium** wing; with [[Joseph Ratzinger]] and von Balthasar founded **Communio** (1972) — a "middle path," theology of **communion**, biblical/patristic priority, caution toward the secular world. John Paul II elevated him (with Ratzinger, von Balthasar) to cardinal to anchor this reading.
