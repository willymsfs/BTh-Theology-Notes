# Karl Rahner, SJ

> [!summary] One-line
> Jesuit peritus; the **transcendental method** and **"anonymous Christianity"** behind **LG 16**'s universal salvific will; read the Council as the birth of the **"World Church"**; co-founded **Concilium**.

**Order:** Jesuit · **Tendency:** progressive neo-Thomist · **Journal:** [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics|Concilium]]

## Background & method
Bridged Catholic theology with modern philosophy. His **transcendental method** shifted theology from the "cosmocentric objective philosophy of the Greeks to the anthropocentric transcendental philosophy of the moderns" — the turn to the human subject, held compatible with Christian principles.

## Key concepts
- **Grace in existence (supernatural existential):** God's grace is already inherently present in the world and the human person — "Two Absolute Mysteries: Christ in History and Grace in Existence."
- **Anonymous Christianity:** radical openness to non-Christians via God's **Universal Salvific Will** — salvation accessible to those who do not explicitly know Christ.
- **World Church:** Vatican II as the Church's move beyond Greco-Roman/European confines into dialogue with global cultures, Islam, Eastern religions.

## Documents shaped
- **[[Lumen Gentium]] §16** — the positive theology of the universal salvific will; Church respects truth beyond its visible boundaries.

## Tensions & post-conciliar trajectory
Part of the "victorious party" at the Council. After 1965, split from the ressourcement wing: called VII "the **beginning of a beginning**," pushed continuous *aggiornamento*. Co-founded **Concilium** (with [[Hans Küng]], [[Edward Schillebeeckx]]). His accommodating trajectory was later viewed with suspicion under John Paul II and Benedict XVI.
