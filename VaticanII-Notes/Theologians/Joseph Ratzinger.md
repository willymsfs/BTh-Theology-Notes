# Joseph Ratzinger (later Pope Benedict XVI)

> [!summary] One-line
> In his early 30s, peritus for **Frings**; ressourcement (Bonaventure, Augustine); shaped [[Dei Verbum]] & [[Lumen Gentium]]; wary of [[Gaudium et Spes]]' "astonishing optimism"; co-founded **Communio**; author of the **2005 hermeneutic of reform** address.

**Role:** peritus for Cardinal **Josef Frings** of Cologne · **Tendency:** neo-Augustinian · **Journal:** [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics|Communio]]

## Foundations
Rooted in **nouvelle théologie** and **ressourcement** — renew the Church by returning to Scripture and the Fathers, not rigid neo-Scholasticism. Postdoctoral work on **St Bonaventure** shaped his anthropology/ecclesiology, setting a dualism between the "kingdom of God" and the "order of history."

## Documents shaped
- **[[Dei Verbum]]** and **[[Lumen Gentium]]** — core dogmatic texts.
- **[[Gaudium et Spes]]** — deep **reservations**: "astonishing optimism," likened to the "naïve optimism" of the Kennedy-era "great society"; parts read like a compliant "revision of the *Syllabus* of Pius IX."

## Post-conciliar & the 2005 address
Split from the liberal majority; with [[Henri de Lubac]] and von Balthasar founded **Communio** (1972) against theologians acting as a "rival Magisterium" / calling for "Vatican III."
As **Benedict XVI (2005 Christmas address to the Curia):** condemned the **"hermeneutic of discontinuity and rupture"**, championed the **"hermeneutic of reform"/continuity** — read VII in line with the Church's 2,000-year tradition. → [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics]]
