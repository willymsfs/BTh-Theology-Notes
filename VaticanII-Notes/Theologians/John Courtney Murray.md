# John Courtney Murray, SJ

> [!summary] One-line
> American Jesuit **censured before the Council**, excluded from session one; brought by Card. Spellman; the **decisive architect of [[Dignitatis Humanae]]** — the religiously **neutral state** and rejection of coercion.

**Order:** American Jesuit · **Signature document:** [[Dignitatis Humanae]]

## Pre-council censorship
In the 1950s his orthodoxy on **church-state relations** was questioned by Roman authorities → **censured** and **excluded from the first session**.

## Role & key ideas
Card. **Francis Spellman** (New York) appointed him peritus, enabling his contributions in the later sessions. Became the **decisive architect** of the Church's revised teaching on **religious freedom**:
- The **religiously neutral state** — the state need not profess the Catholic faith or grant it privileged status.
- **Total rejection of coercion** in the sphere of belief.

## Controversy
Appeared to reverse prior teaching (which had condemned "indifferentism of the state" and treated religious liberty as an evil). Debates were contentious — the **"Black Week"** vote on religious freedom was stalled by conservatives (Nov 1964). Yet his position won overwhelmingly: **[[Dignitatis Humanae]] approved 1,954–249**.

## Legacy
Reshaped the Church's relationship with **secular democracies**; established civil tolerance as a theological necessity. Also the flashpoint for **Lefebvre/SSPX** rejection. → [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics]]
