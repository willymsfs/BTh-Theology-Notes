# Dei Verbum — Dogmatic Constitution on Divine Revelation

> [!summary] One-line
> Revelation as **personal self-gift of God**, not just propositions. Scripture + Tradition = one "single sacred deposit"; the Magisterium **serves**, not rules over, the Word. Vote **2,344–6**. Born from the rejected 1962 schema.

**Type:** Constitution · **Promulgated:** 18 Nov 1965 · **Origin:** the withdrawn "Sources of Revelation" schema, reworked by the mixed commission under [[05 - Theologians Influence Map|Card. Bea]] & Ottaviani.

## Central idea
How God communicates: revelation is a **deeply personal invitation** from God to relationship, received "with reverence" and proclaimed "with faith" (**DV 1**).

## Key themes
- **Scripture, Tradition, Magisterium (DV 7–10):** Tradition and Scripture form "a single sacred deposit of the Word of God" (DV 10). The Magisterium is **"not above the word of God but serves it"** — listening, guarding, explaining (DV 10).
- **Inspiration & inerrancy (DV 11–13):** the Holy Spirit inspired the human authors — God and the writers are both true "authors" (DV 11). The Scriptures "firmly, faithfully and without error, teach that truth which God, for the sake of our salvation, wished to see confided to the Sacred Scriptures" (**DV 11** — salvific inerrancy).

## Key quotes
> *"The study of the sacred page is, so to speak, the soul of sacred theology."* — **DV 24**

> *"Just as from constant attendance at the Eucharistic mystery the life of the Church draws increase, so a new impulse of the spiritual life may be expected from increased veneration of the Word of God."* — **DV 26**

## Structure
Prologue (1) · Divine Revelation (2–6) · Transmission (7–10) · Sacred Scripture (11–13) · Old Testament (14–16) · New Testament (17–20) · Scripture in the Life of the Church (21–26).
