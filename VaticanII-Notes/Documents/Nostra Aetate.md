# Nostra Aetate — Declaration on Non-Christian Religions

> [!summary] One-line
> Common origin and destiny of all humanity in God; the Church "**rejects nothing that is true and holy**" in other religions; on Judaism, **rejects collective "deicide" guilt** and decries anti-Semitism.

**Type:** Declaration · **Promulgated:** 28 Oct 1965 · **Shortest but among the most consequential.**

## Central idea
The Church recognizes the common origin and destiny of all humanity in God, and fosters mutual understanding, respect and dialogue with non-Christian religions.

## Key points by religion
- **Hinduism (NA 2):** deep philosophical inquiry; the divine mystery expressed through myths.
- **Buddhism (NA 2):** realizing the insufficiency of the changeable world; the path to supreme illumination.
- **Islam (NA 3):** esteems Muslims — adoration of the one merciful God, reverence for **Jesus as prophet** (not God), honoring the **Virgin Mary**, prayer/almsgiving/fasting.
- **Judaism (NA 4):** shared spiritual patrimony; Jews **must not be presented as rejected or accursed**; explicitly **decries anti-Semitism**.
- **General (NA 5):** rejects any discrimination based on race, color or religion.

## Key quote
> *"The Catholic Church rejects nothing that is true and holy in these religions. She regards with sincere reverence those ways of conduct and of life, those precepts and teachings which… nonetheless often reflect a ray of that Truth which enlightens all men."* — **NA 2**

## Controversy
Feared Arab-world backlash endangering Middle-East Christians; the "deicide" and anti-Semitism clauses were politically charged. → [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics]]
