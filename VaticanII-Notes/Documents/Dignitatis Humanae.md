# Dignitatis Humanae — Declaration on Religious Liberty

> [!summary] One-line
> Every person has a **right to religious freedom** — immunity from coercion; the state need not profess a "true religion." The Council's most **contested** vote (**1,954–249**) and the flashpoint of the Lefebvre schism.

**Type:** Declaration · **Promulgated:** 7 Dec 1965 · **Chief drafter:** [[05 - Theologians Influence Map|John Courtney Murray]]

## Central idea
Every human person possesses a fundamental **right to religious freedom** and must be immune from coercion by any individual, group or civil power (DH 2).

## Key points
- Major theological shift: the **state does not need to profess a "true religion"** or give it privileged legal status.
- Grounded in the **dignity of the human person**; civil tolerance for all faiths.
- Rejects, on **theological** grounds, coercion in belief — the response to God in faith must be **entirely free**.
- Dialogue with those outside the Church (DH 14).

## Key quote
> *"The Vatican Synod declares that the Human Person has a Right to Religious Freedom. This Freedom means that all People are to be Immune from Coercion on the Part of Individuals or of Social Groups and of any Human Power."* — **DH 2**

## Controversy
- Vote postponed during **"Black Week."**
- **Lefebvre / SSPX** rejected it as a "theological legalization of apostasy" — a chief cause of the traditionalist schism. → [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics]]
- Murray had been **censured** before the Council for these very ideas.
