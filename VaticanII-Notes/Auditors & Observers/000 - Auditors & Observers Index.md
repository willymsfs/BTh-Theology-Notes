# Lay Auditors & Non-Catholic Observers

> [!summary] One-line
> Neither group could vote; both **reshaped the Council** — auditors moved it from a "clerical" Church toward a participatory one, observers seeded a new ecumenical awareness.

## Lay auditors
- [[Jean Guitton]] — **first** lay auditor (invited by John XXIII).
- [[Patrick Keegan]] — **only layperson to address** the assembly.
- **Women:** just before the third period (1964) Paul VI announced women would join — **8 religious + 7 lay women** observers.
- Preparatory commissions initially had "no women and no lay people"; the shift built a **"more participatory Church"** and endorsed the laity's own baptismal apostolate. → [[Apostolicam Actuositatem]]

## Non-Catholic observers
- **40** official observers (1962) → **103** by the final session; total ecumenical guests **180+**.
- Could not vote/speak publicly, but had an "obvious if unofficial" influence via the **Secretariat for Christian Unity** and personal contacts; some acted as unofficial theological advisers on revelation, religious freedom, ecclesiology, ecumenism.
- [[John Moorman]] — chief **Anglican** observer → ARCIC.
- [[Oscar Cullmann]] — **Lutheran**; Scripture rediscovery; Council as "event."
- Others (see [[04 - Participants]]): Edmund Schlink, Karl Barth (Protestant); Orthodox from Moscow then Constantinople.

## Significance
→ endorsed the laity's active apostolate; → fostered new **ecumenical awareness** shaping [[Unitatis Redintegratio]], [[Dei Verbum]], [[Dignitatis Humanae]].
