# Participants: Popes, Bishops, Periti, Laity, Observers

> [!summary] One-line
> **2,908 Council Fathers** from five continents (largest assembly in Church history) — plus theological *periti*, lay auditors (incl. women from 1964), and non-Catholic observers whose presence reshaped the tone.

## The two popes → full profiles: [[000 - Popes Index]]
- [[John XXIII]] — the **"Architect."** Announced the Council (25 Jan 1959). Twin vision: **aggiornamento** + **reunione** (unity). Opening *Gaudet Mater Ecclesia* rejected the "prophets of gloom"; chose the **"medicine of mercy."**
- [[Paul VI]] (formerly Card. Montini) — the **"Master Builder."** Continued after John's death; four purposes (nature of Church, renewal, unity, dialogue). Appointed moderators, expanded lay auditors.

## Leading bishops & cardinals
> [!tip] Full bishop profiles → [[000 - Bishops Index]]

### Majority / reformers ("transalpine")
- **Achille Liénart** (France) & [[Josef Frings]] (Germany) — day-one intervention to elect commissions freely.
- [[Leo Josef Suenens]] (Belgium) — Moderator; *ad intra / ad extra*; championed [[Gaudium et Spes]].
- **Bernard Alfrink** (Netherlands), **Franz König** (Austria), **Julius Döpfner** (Germany).

### Minority / conservatives
- [[Alfredo Ottaviani]] (Holy Office) — defender of traditional, juridical, defensive ecclesiology; led Doctrinal Commission; clashed with Frings.
- **Giuseppe Siri**, **Ernesto Ruffini** — opposed major reforms.
- [[Marcel Lefebvre]] — *Coetus Internationalis Patrum*; later SSPX schism.

## Periti (theological experts) — full map in [[05 - Theologians Influence Map]]
They could **not vote** but shaped drafting. Two tendencies:
- **Neo-Thomist / progressive:** [[Yves Congar]], **Marie-Dominique Chenu**, **Edward Schillebeeckx**, **Karl Rahner**; **Hans Küng** (prominent liberal voice).
- **Neo-Augustinian / ressourcement:** **Joseph Ratzinger** (peritus for Frings), **Henri de Lubac**, **Jean Daniélou** — reformers who later resisted radical post-conciliar liberalism.
- **John Courtney Murray** — decisive on [[Dignitatis Humanae]].
- **Gérard Philips** — joint secretary of Doctrinal Commission; chief redactor of [[Lumen Gentium]].

## Lay auditors (incl. women) → full profiles: [[000 - Auditors & Observers Index]]
- John XXIII first invited one layman ([[Jean Guitton]]); Paul VI added ten more from international Catholic organizations.
- [[Patrick Keegan]] (English lay leader) — the only layperson ever to **address the assembly** (on the laity schema). → [[Apostolicam Actuositatem]]
- Third period (1964): **8 religious women + 7 lay women** observers invited.
- **Gertrud Heinzelmann** petitioned from outside: *We Won't Keep Silence Any Longer: Women Speak Out to Vatican Council II.*

## Non-Catholic observers (could not speak/vote, but influenced tone) → [[Unitatis Redintegratio]]
- **Anglican:** [[John Moorman]] (Bp of Ripon) — *"Rome has, at last, begun to interest herself in the problem of unity, and things can never be the same again."*
- **Lutheran/Protestant:** **Edmund Schlink**; [[Oscar Cullmann]] (praised the rediscovery of Scripture); **Karl Barth** (invited, ill; analyzed texts closely).
- **Eastern Orthodox:** first only the Moscow Patriarchate; Constantinople sent observers by the third session. **Olivier Clément** (liturgy), **Nikos Nissiotis** (welcomed ecumenism, critiqued "ecclesial paternalism").
