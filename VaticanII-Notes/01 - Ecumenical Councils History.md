# History, Purpose & Significance of the Ecumenical Councils

> [!summary] One-line
> An ecumenical council is a worldwide assembly of bishops, convened and ratified by the Pope, that defines doctrine and orders Church life; there have been **21**, from Nicaea (325) to Vatican II (1962–65).

## What makes a council "ecumenical"
An ecumenical council gathers bishops, patriarchs, cardinals and hierarchs from the whole world to discuss faith and discipline. To be **valid and truly ecumenical** it must be:
- **Convened and presided over** by the Roman Pontiff or his legates, and
- Its decisions **approved and ratified by the pope**.

Pope John XXIII called councils a *"solemn celebration of the union of Christ and His Church."*

**Classic purpose:** define doctrines/dogmas, denounce heresies by anathemas, and state the official teaching of the Church.

## The 21 Councils — historical eras
| Era | Councils |
|---|---|
| Antiquity | 1–8 (early Church) |
| Papal / Middle Ages | 9–15 (discipline, defense of Christendom) |
| Conciliarism + Lateran V | 16, 17, 18 |
| Modern | 19 Trent · 20 Vatican I · 21 Vatican II |

**Greatest weight:** the first four + the last three.

### The first four (Christological/Trinitarian core)
- **Nicaea I (325):** Nicene Creed; Father and Son of the *same substance*; rejects **Arianism**.
- **Constantinople I (381):** Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed; divinity of the **Holy Spirit**.
- **Ephesus (431):** Mary as **Theotokos** (Mother of God); rejects **Nestorianism**.
- **Chalcedon (451):** Christ **two natures**, perfect God and perfect man, one person, "without confusion or separation."

## The two modern predecessors
**Council of Trent (1545–1563)** — response to the Protestant Reformation. Rigidly defined Scripture & Tradition, original sin, justification, sacraments; produced the Tridentine Mass (1570). A defensive, doctrine-defining council.

**Vatican I (1869–1870)** — reaction against modern liberalism, the French Revolution, Marxism, historical-critical skepticism. A "fortress" council; produced *Pastor Aeternus*, dogmatizing **papal infallibility** and centralizing authority. Cut short by the invasion of the Papal States — its work on the nature of the Church left **unfinished** (Vatican II would complete it).

## How Vatican II differs — see [[02 - Vatican II Origin Purpose & Context]]
- **Pastoral, not condemnatory** — no new dogmas, no anathemas; the "**Medicine of Mercy**."
- **A world-church** — 2,500–2,900 bishops from five continents (Vatican I: ~700, mostly European) → first truly global council.
- **Ressourcement + aggiornamento** — engage modernity instead of building a fortress.
- **Collegiality** — the college of bishops *with* the pope shares supreme power (balances Vatican I's centralization). → [[Lumen Gentium]]
- **Continuity over rupture** — John XXIII framed it as continuing Trent & Vatican I; "certain and immutable doctrine" kept, but "reformulated in contemporary terms." → [[06 - Reception Controversies & Hermeneutics]]

> [!quote] Key image
> Vatican II transformed the Church's self-understanding from a defensive **"fortress"** into a **"pilgrim people."** The Church is *semper purificanda* — "simultaneously holy and always in need of purification."
