# Reception, Acceptance/Rejection & Controversies

> [!summary] One-line
> The Council's texts were fought over during ("Black Week," *Nota Praevia*) and after — the **hermeneutic of continuity/reform** (Benedict XVI) vs the **hermeneutic of rupture**, the **Concilium/Communio/Bologna** schools, and outright rejection by **Lefebvre/SSPX**.

## During the Council: the conservative minority
Fierce minority opposition on liturgy, religious freedom, ecumenism, the nature of the Church.

> [!warning] "Black Week" (14–21 Nov 1964)
> Three interventions that frustrated the majority:
> 1. **Nota explicativa praevia** inserted into [[Lumen Gentium]] — reassured conservatives that **collegiality** was not "conciliarism," reinforcing **papal primacy**.
> 2. Vote on **religious freedom** ([[Dignitatis Humanae]]) **postponed**.
> 3. Paul VI introduced modifications to the **ecumenism** decree without discussion.

## After the Council: the hermeneutical debate
Benedict XVI (2005) framed the core conflict:
- **Hermeneutic of discontinuity / rupture** — pre- and post-conciliar Church as two different churches; the "true" council is its trajectory, not its (compromised) texts. #hermeneutic-of-rupture
- **Hermeneutic of reform / continuity** — read the Council in light of the whole 2,000-year Tradition. #hermeneutic-of-continuity

### Competing schools & journals
| School / Journal | Figures | Stance |
|---|---|---|
| **Concilium** (1965) | Rahner, Küng, Schillebeeckx | Perpetuate the reforming "spirit"; some called for a "Vatican III." |
| **Communio** (1972) | Ratzinger, de Lubac, von Balthasar | "Middle path" — embrace *ressourcement*, resist radical liberal accommodation; theologians are not an alternative magisterium. |
| **Bologna School** | Giuseppe Alberigo (*History of Vatican II*) | Council as epoch-making **"event"**; critics charged it favored the **"spirit"** over the texts. |

## The traditionalist rejection: Lefebvre / SSPX
- **Marcel Lefebvre** founded the **Society of St Pius X (SSPX)** in 1970; saw VII as the "errors" of the modern world entering the Church — a "liberal-Protestant-Masonic conspiracy."
- Rejected [[Dignitatis Humanae]] as a **"theological legalization of apostasy."**
- Condemned the liturgical reform; defended the Latin Mass; called the new Mass "Luther's Mass."
- **1988:** consecrated four bishops against papal orders → **automatic excommunication / schism.**

## Gaudium et Spes — the optimism dispute → [[Gaudium et Spes]]
- **Neo-Augustinians** (Ratzinger, de Lubac, von Balthasar): GS breathes an "astonishing optimism," too accommodating, neglects sin and the cross; a naïve "revision of the *Syllabus*."
- **Neo-Thomists** (Chenu, Congar): incarnational embrace of history and the "signs of the times."

## Nostra Aetate & Jewish relations → [[Nostra Aetate]]
- Feared Arab-world backlash endangering Middle-East Christians.
- Final text: shared spiritual patrimony, **rejects collective "deicide" guilt**, decries all anti-Semitism.

## "Spirit of the Council" debate
- Progressives appealed to the **"spirit"** to push beyond the texts.
- Benedict XVI (2005) condemned this as making "room for every whim."
- Lefebvrists also invoked a "spirit" — but as a **negative** modernist infiltration.
